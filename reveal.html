PSYCHE

What Psyche sees

The understanding underneath your conversation.

The pattern

Avoidance to prevent a feared outcome.

You keep the decision open so you never have to face being wrong about it. Staying undecided feels safer than choosing — but it's the avoidance itself, not the choice, that keeps you stuck.

Root cause

Fear that a wrong choice means being a failure.

Underneath is a belief that your worth rides on getting it right. So a decision stops being about the job and becomes a verdict on you — which makes any choice feel dangerous.

Feeling-tone

Unpleasant → aversion (pushing the decision away).

The thought of deciding carries an unpleasant feeling, and the mind reacts by pushing it away. Naming that reaction — aversion — is the first step to meeting it instead of obeying it.

Wisdom

What we resist persists; what we meet, passes.

A feeling met with steady attention rises, crests, and fades. A feeling resisted only waits. The way out is through — turning toward the discomfort rather than around it.

The way through

Steps to work through, in order.

1

Practise small choices daily

Each morning, make one small choice within a minute — what to eat, which task first. Training that choosing is survivable.

Decision-fear shrinks with repetition. Tiny, low-stakes choices teach your nervous system that choosing doesn't end in catastrophe — so the big choice feels less charged.
2

Answer the failure-thought

When it arises, meet it: "A wrong turn is information, not a verdict." Repeat until it loosens.

The belief "wrong = failure" runs unexamined. Meeting it with a truer thought, repeatedly, loosens its grip — not by arguing, but by offering the mind a kinder, more accurate frame.
3

Sit with the discomfort 60 seconds

Find the feeling in your body. Don't fix it — watch it rise and pass.

This is the core practice: meeting the feeling-tone without reacting. Sixty seconds of steady attention shows you directly that the discomfort moves through — you don't have to push it away.
